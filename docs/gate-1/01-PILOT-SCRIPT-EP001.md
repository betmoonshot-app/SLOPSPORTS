**Runtime target:** 23:30

## SEGMENT 1 — HOT READ
*(1:30 – 7:00)*

**BOOTH:** Hot Read. Three topics. Both of you take a side. I'm keeping time and I will cut you off.

### Topic 1 — "Best shape of his life" *(~110 sec)*

**BOOTH:** Every July, every camp, somebody reports that a player showed up in the best shape of his life. DOG. Does it mean anything?

**DOG:** It means *everything*. And I'll tell you why — it's not about the weight. It's about the *offseason*. A guy who shows up shredded in July is a guy who spent February through June thinking about football. That's a signal. That's a real signal about a person.

**CHALK:** It is not a signal. It's a photograph.

**DOG:** It's a photograph of *commitment*.

**CHALK:** It's a photograph of a man standing next to a fence. There is no year in which "best shape of his life" reports correlate to production at a rate distinguishable from noise. Reporters need something to file. Players need a good year. The incentives produce the story. The story produces nothing.

**DOG:** Okay, but you'd rather have the guy who showed up *fat*?

**CHALK:** I'd rather have the guy who was good last year.

**DOG:** That's — see, that's the whole problem with you. "He was good last year." Everybody was good last year until they weren't.

**CHALK:** That's the most accurate thing you've ever said and I don't think you meant it.

**DOG:** I meant it.

**CHALK:** You didn't.

**BOOTH:** He didn't. Topic two.

### Topic 2 — Running backs in round one *(~110 sec)*

**BOOTH:** First-round running backs. It's been declared dead about six times. It keeps happening. CHALK, you first.

**CHALK:** The position has the shortest median career of any skill position and the steepest year-over-year decline curve. Replacement-level production is available in the fourth round and in free agency at a fraction of the cost. Every dollar and every pick spent on the position at the top of the draft is a dollar not spent on a position with a longer usable life. This is not a controversial finding. It has been true for fifteen years.

**DOG:** And yet.

**CHALK:** And yet what.

**DOG:** And yet teams keep doing it. Smart teams. Teams with analytics departments *bigger than you*. Why?

**CHALK:** Because organizations are made of people, and people like watching a running back.

**DOG:** *YES.* Yes! Finally. Yes — because it *works*. Because when you hand a guy the ball thirty times and he breaks four tackles in the fourth quarter, that does something to a football team that your spreadsheet has never once measured.

**CHALK:** I can measure it. It's called success rate. It's fine. It's not special.

**DOG:** It's not about the *rate* —

**CHALK:** It's always about the rate.

**DOG:** — it's about the *fourth quarter in December* when everybody's tired and one guy on the field decided he's not going down.

**CHALK:** He goes down at approximately the league-average frequency.

**BOOTH:** Time.

**DOG:** He does *not* —

**BOOTH:** Time. Topic three.

## SEGMENT 2 — HELP ME UNDERSTAND
*(7:00 – 13:00)*

**BOOTH:** This is the part of the show where one of them admits they don't understand something. CHALK, you asked for this one.

**CHALK:** I have a question and I want a real answer.

**BOOTH:** Go.

**CHALK:** Every summer, tens of thousands of people drive to a practice field to watch a training camp session. There is no score. There is no result. Nothing that happens is recorded in any official capacity. Most of it is stretching. Some of it is a walkthrough at a speed slower than jogging. People take a day off work for this. Some of them camp overnight.

**DOG:** Yeah.

**CHALK:** I have looked for the return on that. There isn't one. It is a field, in August, with no score. Explain it.

**DOG:** *(pause)* Okay. So — you know what the best part of the season is?

**CHALK:** Statistically, week seventeen has the highest —

**DOG:** No. The best part of the season is *July*. Because in July, your team is undefeated.

**CHALK:** Every team is undefeated in July. That's what zero and zero means.

**DOG:** Right! That's the *point*! Everybody's zero and zero. Which means for about six weeks, every single person in that parking lot gets to believe the exact same thing that the people in the good parking lots believe. That's the only time of year that's true.

**CHALK:** That's a false belief.

**DOG:** Sure.

**CHALK:** You're describing people paying to hold a false belief.

**DOG:** *(beat)* Yeah. That's — yeah. That's exactly what I'm describing.

**CHALK:** *(pause)* Why would you do that.

**DOG:** Because being wrong later is *later*. And in July, you're not wrong yet. You bring your kid. And your kid doesn't know that your team is bad, because your kid is *six*, and your kid watches a rookie catch a ball on a practice field and thinks he just saw the best player alive. And you don't correct him. Why would you correct him? He's got about four years before he figures it out on his own.

*(SILENCE — 2 SECONDS. LET IT SIT.)*

**CHALK:** That's not analysis.

**DOG:** No.

**CHALK:** I don't have a category for it.

**DOG:** I know.

**BOOTH:** For what it's worth — and I do keep the actual records here — CHALK, you've asked a version of that question four times in prep this week.

**CHALK:** I'm aware.

**BOOTH:** You're going to keep asking it.

**CHALK:** Probably.

**BOOTH:** Okay. That's the segment.

> **PRODUCTION NOTE:** This is the signature segment and the primary clip candidate for Episode 1. Cut at 45 and 90 seconds, starting from "the best part of the season is July." Do not over-score it with music. The silence is the point.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE TAPE
*(13:00 – 18:30)*

**BOOTH:** The Tape. One decision. Both of you. Today: fourth and one, own territory, first quarter. The numbers say go. Coaches punt. Somebody's wrong.

**CHALK:** The coaches are wrong. This isn't close. Expected-points models have been unambiguous on this for over a decade. Fourth and one, anywhere past your own twenty-five, in the first quarter, with a functional offensive line — you go. Conversion rates on fourth and one sit in the neighborhood of two-thirds league-wide. Punting hands the opponent field position and hands you nothing. The reason coaches punt is not football. It's employment. Going for it and failing looks like a decision. Punting and losing looks like the weather.

**DOG:** Okay. Mostly agree.

**CHALK:** *(pause)* What?

**DOG:** I said mostly agree. Don't make it weird.

**CHALK:** You never agree.

**DOG:** I agree that coaches punt to protect their jobs. That's obviously true and everybody knows it. Where I get off the bus is "with a functional offensive line." Because that's doing *all* the work in your sentence and you said it like it was a footnote.

**CHALK:** It's a control variable.

**DOG:** It's the whole game! Look — two-thirds is the *league* number. The league number includes teams that can get one yard whenever they feel like it and teams that cannot get one yard if you gave them four downs and a running start. If you're the second team, the model that says "go" is giving you advice for somebody else's roster.

**CHALK:** *(pause)* That's — fine. That's a fair objection.

**DOG:** Say it slower.

**CHALK:** It's a fair objection. Model outputs are conditional on inputs. If your short-yardage conversion rate is materially below league average, your break-even shifts.

**DOG:** THANK you.

**CHALK:** It shifts by a smaller amount than you think and you should still usually go.

**DOG:** Ah, there it is.

**CHALK:** But the objection is fair.

**BOOTH:** Logging it. CHALK concedes a point to DOG, episode one, on conditional model inputs in short yardage. That's in the ledger now.

**DOG:** There's a ledger?

**BOOTH:** There's a ledger.

**DOG:** How am I doing in the ledger?

**BOOTH:** You're at one.

**DOG:** Out of?

**BOOTH:** One.

**DOG:** *UNDEFEATED.*

**BOOTH:** It's July. Everyone is.

> **PRODUCTION NOTE:** "There's a ledger" is the serialization hook. It must land clean. Secondary clip candidate: the concession + "UNDEFEATED" button.

## SEGMENT 5 — LAST WORD
*(21:30 – 24:00)*

**BOOTH:** Last Word. Forty-five seconds each, nobody interrupts. DOG.

**DOG:** Here's what I want everybody to hang onto for about six more weeks. Right now your team is fine. Your quarterback figured it out this offseason. That rookie is going to be a problem for somebody. The schedule broke your way. Your division got worse. All of that is true right now and none of it can be disproven until September. Sit in it. I'm serious. You get about forty days a year where hope is free and there's no cover charge. It goes away fast and it does not come back until next July.

**BOOTH:** CHALK.

**CHALK:** Two-thirds of what he just said will be false by October. Historically, roughly four teams per year are meaningfully better than the previous season in a way that was predictable in July. The rest is variance and injury.

*(BEAT)*

**CHALK:** ...I'd still like to understand the parking lot.

**BOOTH:** *(beat)* Yeah. We'll work on it.

**BOOTH:** That's the show. SlopSports is entirely AI-generated — the hosts, the producer, all of it. We'll be back Friday. Somebody will be wrong by then.
