# SLOPSPORTS — Written Content Engine v1.0

**Status:** Gate 1 addition. Not approved for production.
**Scope:** The site's written layer — reaction posts, columns, and the ledger.

---

## 0. The thesis

The written layer is not a marketing channel for the podcast. It is the **bedrock of the enterprise**, and it should be built as such from the first post.

The durable subject of SlopSports is not football. It is **how an artificial intelligence interprets human behavior** — and sports is the beachhead domain because it's where humans behave most legibly and least rationally. Grown adults paint their bodies. Cities grieve. People assign moral character to a man's fourth-down decisions. Every irrational thing humans do about anything, they do about sports, in public, on a schedule, with a scoreboard attached.

That makes it the ideal first domain for a body of work about machine interpretation of human action. It is not the only one.

**What this means practically:**

- **"Help Me Understand" is the flagship of the whole enterprise**, not a segment of a podcast. It's the format that carries the thesis, the one that travels furthest outside sports audiences, and the one that would survive a move into any other domain.
- **The archive is the asset.** A single post is disposable. Four hundred posts, tracking one intelligence's slowly deepening comprehension of why people care about things, is a body of work — and it compounds in a way that no individual piece of content does.
- **Sports is the beachhead, not the ceiling.** Nothing in the character design, the column structure, or the editorial standards is football-specific. CHALK asking why people drive to a training camp is the same question as CHALK asking why people queue overnight for a product launch, or why a city mourns a building. Year 1 is NFL. That is a focus decision, not an identity.
- **Correctness matters more than volume, permanently.** An enterprise built on interpretation has exactly one asset — that its interpretations are honest and its facts are right. The ledger, the public corrections, and the human review requirement are not overhead. They are the product.

Everything below is in service of that. The volume restraint, the no-listicles rule, the review checklist — those aren't defensive measures against being mistaken for spam. They're what building a body of work instead of an inventory actually requires.

## 1. The sequencing argument

**Recommendation: the blog launches before the podcast, not alongside it.**

Reasoning:

| | Blog post | Podcast episode |
|---|---|---|
| Production time | ~45–75 min | ~8 hrs |
| Marginal cost | ~$0.50 in API | ~$12 in TTS + 8 hrs |
| Tests the characters? | Yes | Yes |
| Tests the voices? | No | Yes |
| Can be corrected after publishing? | Yes | Effectively no |

The characters are the risky part of this product. The blog tests them at roughly a tenth the cost, with reversible mistakes, and builds a standing audience for the show to launch into.

**Proposed order:**
1. **Blog launches first** — 6–8 weeks of written content, 4–5 posts/week
2. **Podcast launches into an existing readership**, with the blog as its permanent home
3. Blog becomes the show's connective tissue between episodes

This also fixes the discovery gap flagged in the earlier review. A twice-weekly podcast goes quiet for three days at a time. The blog keeps the brand publishing daily without the hours that daily audio would demand.

## 2. The risk that has to be designed around

Two things are true and both matter:

**Google's March 2024 core and spam updates explicitly target "scaled content abuse"** — mass-produced content generated primarily to manipulate rankings, whether AI-written or not. High-volume AI sports content is close to the archetype of what that policy is aimed at.

**Sports Illustrated (2023) and CNET (2023) were both publicly damaged** for publishing AI-generated articles — SI notably under fabricated author bylines with AI-generated headshots. The reputational damage substantially exceeded the traffic gained.

### Why SlopSports is a different thing — if it's built as one

The failure mode in both cases was **AI content pretending to be human content**. SI invented writers. CNET buried the disclosure. The deception was the story, not the AI.

SlopSports inverts this entirely:

- The bylines are **CHALK**, **DOG**, and **BOOTH**. Nobody could mistake them for people.
- Every post carries an AI-generated label.
- The show is called *SlopSports*.
- The content is **first-person commentary from a named character**, not anonymous informational articles.

That last point is the load-bearing one. "CHALK reacts to the fourth-down decision" is a **column**. "The 10 Best NFL Running Backs of 2026" is **SEO inventory**. The first has an editorial voice and a reason to exist. The second is what the spam policy is for.

### The rules that follow

1. **No generic SEO inventory.** No listicles, no "how to watch," no "what time is kickoff," no evergreen ranking pages. Ever. These are the highest-risk, lowest-value content on the internet and they are exactly what SlopSports must not produce.
2. **Every post is in-character and first-person.** If a post could have been written by any site, it doesn't get published.
3. **Volume stays deliberately restrained.** 4–5 posts/week, not 40. This is the single most important rule and the one most at odds with the instinct to generate.
4. **Every post gets human review before publishing.** Non-negotiable — see §6.
5. **Never fabricate a quote, a source, a statistic, or a byline.** The SI failure was fabricated humans. Ours is a brand built on not doing that.
6. **Don't plan on Google.** Build for the audience you have and for social sharing. Treat search traffic as a bonus that may never arrive. A strategy that depends on ranking is a strategy dependent on a policy that's actively hostile to it.

## 3. The columns

Five recurring formats. Each is a container, not a one-off.

### "THE NUMBER" — CHALK | 250–400 words | 2×/week
One statistic, examined coldly, usually to demonstrate that a popular belief is wrong. Short by design. Ends on a flat, devastating line.

**Why it works:** highly shareable, low production cost, and it's the cheapest possible demonstration that the site knows football.

### "HELP ME UNDERSTAND" — CHALK | 600–900 words | 1×/week
The written version of the signature segment. CHALK takes something about football *culture* it doesn't comprehend and works at it in public. Sincere, never snarky.

**Why it works:** this is the flagship. It's the piece that gets shared outside sports audiences, and it's the one no other site can produce. It's also the column that carries the season-long comprehension arc.

### "THE TAKE" — DOG | 400–700 words | 2×/week
Full-throated conviction. No hedging, no both-sides. DOG is often wrong and always certain.

**Why it works:** it's the reaction engine. Fast turnaround on news, high emotional register, most likely to be quote-tweeted in anger — which is distribution.

### "THE LEDGER" — BOOTH | 300–500 words | 1×/week
The running scorecard. Who conceded what, who's been proven wrong since, what's still open. BOOTH's dry weekly accounting.

**Why it works:** this is the serialization anchor and the trust mechanic in one. A site that publicly logs its own wrong calls is doing something almost no sports outlet does. It's also structurally impossible to copy without having run the show for months.

### "THE ARGUMENT" — CHALK + DOG | 800–1,200 words | 1×/week
A written debate. One question, alternating sections, BOOTH's one-paragraph ruling at the end.

**Why it works:** it's the podcast format in text, so it converts readers into listeners. Best candidate for the site's most-linked page.

### Weekly volume

| Column | Posts/week |
|---|---|
| The Number | 2 |
| The Take | 2 |
| Help Me Understand | 1 |
| The Ledger | 1 |
| The Argument | 1 |
| **Total** | **7/week** — trim to 4–5 in the first month |

Start at 4–5. Add volume only after the pipeline and the quality bar are both proven.

## 5. Reaction content — the fast lane

This is what you specifically want, so it gets its own treatment.

**Reaction posts run inside "The Take" and "The Number."** They don't need a new column — they need a **turnaround target**.

**Target: publish within 90 minutes of a news event.**

The pipeline:

1. News breaks. Facts are confirmed from at least two reputable outlets and linked.
2. Route it to the right character:
   - Contract, trade, roster math → **CHALK** (The Number)
   - Anything emotional, controversial, or fan-facing → **DOG** (The Take)
   - Anything where the site has previously been wrong → **BOOTH** (The Ledger)
3. Draft generated against the character's system prompt with the confirmed facts as context.
4. **Human review** — facts, tone, guardrails.
5. Publish. Pull two quotes for social.

**The hard rules for reaction content:**

- **Facts must come from linked, reputable reporting.** The site has no sources and says so.
- **No speculation on injuries, health, contracts, or personal matters** beyond what's officially confirmed.
- **No reacting to breaking news involving death, serious injury, arrest, or personal tragedy.** Not with these characters, not in this voice, not ever. Route those to silence. This is the single most important editorial rule on the site and it needs to be enforced by the pipeline, not by judgment at 11pm.
- **When the story is thin, don't post.** The instinct to feed the machine is the thing that produces slop.

---

## 6. Human review is not optional

Every post gets reviewed before publishing. Not spot-checked — reviewed.

**The checklist:**

- [ ] Every factual claim is verifiable and linked
- [ ] No fabricated quotes, sources, or statistics
- [ ] No speculation beyond officially confirmed reporting
- [ ] Subject matter clears the editorial guardrails (show bible §11)
- [ ] Voice is correct for the character
- [ ] AI disclosure present
- [ ] Post could not have been written by a generic sports site

**Time cost: ~15–20 minutes per post.** At 5 posts/week that's ~1.5 hours weekly. That's the real cost of the content engine and it should be in the model.

The moment review gets skipped to hit volume, this becomes the thing the earlier review warned about.

## 8. What the blog changes in the revenue model

Modest direct revenue, meaningful indirect:

- **Display ads:** not worth running below ~50K monthly pageviews. Don't bother in Year 1.
- **Newsletter:** the blog's real output. Email is the only audience channel nobody can take away — not Google, not a platform. **Capture email from day one.** A list of 2,000 engaged readers is worth more than 200,000 pageviews.
- **Premium conversion:** the revenue model identified Premium conversion as the highest-leverage lever. The blog is the best conversion surface available — a reader who's read six posts converts far better than a listener who heard one episode.
- **Podcast discovery:** every post carries a player embed.

**The blog's job is not revenue. It's audience, and specifically email addresses.**

## 9. Where this sits in the gates

The blog gets its own gate, ahead of podcast production:

**GATE 1.5 — WRITTEN FORMAT VALIDATION**

Deliverables:
1. Character system prompts for CHALK, DOG, BOOTH (written voice)
2. 10 finished posts across all five columns
3. Editorial standards doc + review checklist
4. Site build — static, fast, five columns, email capture
5. Publishing pipeline documented end-to-end

**Pass criteria:**
- You'd read a second post from each character
- A real NFL fan finds the football credible
- End-to-end production of one post takes under 40 minutes
- Nothing published in the first 10 posts required a correction

**If Gate 1.5 fails, do not proceed to Gate 2.** Characters that don't work in text will not be rescued by voice.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Site platform.** Static generator (Astro/Hugo) is fastest and cheapest. Needs a decision before Gate 1.5.
2. **Does the blog get its own launch,** or launch quietly and build a back catalog before any promotion? *Recommendation: quiet, 3–4 weeks of back catalog first. Nothing looks worse than an empty archive.*
3. **Comments?** *Recommendation: no. Moderation load, toxicity exposure, near-zero benefit. Send discussion to social.*
4. **Newsletter from day one or later?** *Recommendation: day one. It's the only durable channel.*
