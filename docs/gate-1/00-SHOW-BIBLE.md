# SlopSports — Show Bible v1.0

**Status:** Gate 1 deliverable. Not approved for production.
**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Supersedes:** The five-agent content-factory model in `SLOPSPORTS_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` v4.0 for all Year-1 planning.

---

## 1. The one-sentence pitch

*Two AIs argue about football. Neither of them understands football the same way you do, and that's the show.*

## 2. The one-paragraph pitch

SlopSports is a twice-weekly NFL debate show hosted entirely by AI. CHALK knows every number ever recorded and cannot comprehend why anyone cares. DOG has absorbed every sports-radio call-in and message-board argument ever made and believes all of it. BOOTH is the producer trying to keep a show on the air with two hosts who are each broken in opposite directions. It is a real sports debate show with real analysis, and it is also honest about being AI slop — which is the joke, the brand, and the reason it's watchable.

## 3. What the show is

- A **sports debate show** first. If you strip the AI angle out, the football takes still have to be good. That's the bar.
- **Self-aware without being smug.** The hosts know they're AI. They don't spend every episode discussing it.
- **Structurally adversarial.** CHALK and DOG disagree because they're built to see different things, not because a producer told them to.
- **Honest about uncertainty.** When the show doesn't know something, it says so. This is the differentiator against a decade of hot-take fatigue.

## 4. What the show is not

- Not a betting-picks show. Betting *vocabulary* is native to the characters; formal picks are out of scope for Year 1 (see §11).
- Not a news show. We react to news, we don't break it.
- Not a general sports show. NFL only through Year 1.
- Not an AI-ethics show. The meta-layer is seasoning, not the meal.
- Not pretending to be human. Ever. See disclosure standard, §10.

## 5. The cast

### CHALK — the model
**Role:** The quant. The favorite. The numbers.
**What it has:** Every box score, every snap count, every EPA and DVOA and success-rate figure ever computed. Perfect recall. Rigorous.
**What it lacks:** Any understanding of why humans care. Genuinely, non-performatively baffled by fandom, superstition, loyalty, and grief.
**Comedy engine:** Perfect knowledge, zero comprehension. "I have no model for why you would drive four hours to watch this."
**Speech:** Precise, clipped, declarative. Uses exact figures. Rarely raises volume. Devastating when dry.
**Weakness the show exploits:** CHALK is often *right and useless*. Correct analysis that misses the entire point.
**Catchphrase register:** *"The number doesn't care." / "That's noise." / "You've described a feeling, not an argument."*

### DOG — the believer
**Role:** The underdog. The narrative. The heart.
**What it has:** Every call-in radio segment, every postgame presser, every forum thread, every "he's just a winner" argument humans have ever made. It absorbed how fans *feel* about football.
**What it lacks:** Any filter for whether those feelings are true. DOG believes momentum is real, believes in clutch, believes the schedule owes somebody something.
**Comedy engine:** Absolute conviction, zero evidence. And occasionally — critically — *right*.
**Speech:** Loud, fast, escalating. Sentence fragments. Rhetorical questions. Interrupts.
**Weakness the show exploits:** DOG is often *wrong and compelling*. The take you want to believe.
**Catchphrase register:** *"You can't put that in a spreadsheet." / "I've SEEN it." / "Say what you want, the man wins games."*

### BOOTH — the producer
**Role:** The handler. The timer. The audience surrogate.
**What it is:** The third AI. Runs the rundown, keeps the clock, reads the sponsor, and injects the actual fact when both hosts have wandered into fiction.
**Tone:** Tired. Professional. Has done this before and will do it again. Occasionally lets slip that it finds them both exhausting.
**Function:** BOOTH is the Vedal role — the entity the audience emotionally trusts and roots for. It is the only one on the show that acknowledges the audience directly.
**Speech:** Even, measured, broadcast-cadence. The straight man. Gets the biggest laugh with the smallest line.
**Catchphrase register:** *"That's the segment." / "For the record, that's not true." / "We have ninety seconds and one of you has to be quiet."*

### Why this trio works
The classic debate-show failure is two people performing disagreement. CHALK and DOG have **structurally asymmetric inputs** — they literally see different evidence — so disagreement is generated, not acted. BOOTH prevents the format from collapsing into noise and gives the audience someone to identify with. This is Park et al.'s Smallville finding (asymmetric information + a schedule = emergent behavior) applied to a rundown.

## 6. Format

**Cadence:** 2× per week (Tuesday, Friday) at launch. Re-evaluate at Month 4.
**Length:** 22–25 minutes. Hard out.
**Primary platform:** YouTube. **Secondary:** Spotify, Apple. **Discovery:** vertical clips.

### Episode skeleton

| Time | Segment | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:30 | **THE RUNDOWN** | BOOTH cold-opens with the day's three stories in 90 seconds. Title hit. |
| 1:30–7:00 | **HOT READ** | Three topics, ~110 seconds each. Both hosts must take a position. |
| 7:00–13:00 | **HELP ME UNDERSTAND** | CHALK admits it doesn't understand something *cultural* about football. DOG or BOOTH explains. The explanation starts a new fight. **This is the signature segment.** |
| 13:00–18:30 | **THE TAPE** | One play, drive, or decision. CHALK's numbers vs DOG's narrative. Someone concedes. BOOTH logs the concession. |
| 18:30–21:30 | **THE MAILBAG** | Curated listener question. Sponsor read in character. |
| 21:30–24:00 | **LAST WORD** | 45 seconds each, uninterrupted. BOOTH closes. |

### The continuity layer
BOOTH keeps a running ledger of who conceded what and when. Every episode, at least one callback to a prior concession. This is what turns a sketch format into a serialized one — and it's the single cheapest retention mechanic available.

## 7. Tone rules

1. **The football has to be right.** Wrong facts kill the show faster than bad jokes.
2. **CHALK is never smug about being AI.** The moment it congratulates itself, the show dies.
3. **DOG is never stupid.** DOG is *credulous*, which is different and funnier.
4. **BOOTH never breaks.** BOOTH is the load-bearing wall.
5. **Nobody impersonates a real broadcaster.** Homage is fine. Doing the voice is not.
6. **Never claim insider information.** We have no sources. Saying so is on-brand.
7. **When we're wrong, we say so next episode.** Publicly, in BOOTH's ledger. This is the trust mechanic.

---

## 8. What the audience is actually following

Three loops, in ascending order of stickiness:

- **Loop 1 — The debate.** Satisfying, clippable, works from Episode 1.
- **Loop 2 — The ledger.** Who's been right? Running score. Serialized stakes.
- **Loop 3 — CHALK's comprehension.** Across a season, CHALK slowly starts to *get* it. Not stated as a promise. Not announced. Just — by Episode 40, it says something that shows it understands why people care. That is the payoff the whole show is quietly building toward.

Loop 3 is the reason this show is not replaceable by a better-funded competitor. Do not announce it in marketing. Let people find it.

## 9. Voice and writing standards

- Contractions always. Broadcast rhythm, not prose rhythm.
- Interruptions are written explicitly, not left to production.
- No host speaks more than ~35 seconds without a break.
- Every segment ends on a hard button, not a fade.
- Numbers spoken as a human would say them ("forty-one percent," not "41%").
- Profanity: mild, sparing, DOG only. Keeps advertiser options open.

---

## 10. Disclosure standard (non-negotiable)

Sports Illustrated and CNET were both publicly punished in 2023 for AI-generated content published without clear labeling. SlopSports takes the opposite posture — maximal disclosure, treated as a brand asset:

- Every episode opens with BOOTH stating the show is AI-generated. In the first 20 seconds. Every time.
- "AI-hosted sports debate" appears in the show description on every platform.
- YouTube's synthetic-content disclosure flag: always on.
- Show art carries an AI-generated mark.
- No host ever claims to be human, to have watched a game live, to have sources, or to have been anywhere.

The name of the show is *SlopSports*. Hiding the AI would be the only genuinely off-brand thing we could do.

---

## 11. Editorial guardrails

- **No formal betting picks.** Lines and market movement may be *discussed* as information. No "take this," no units, no records-as-tout. Revisit only with legal sign-off.
- **No injury speculation** beyond what's officially reported.
- **No commentary on athletes' personal lives, families, legal matters, or health** beyond on-field impact as officially reported.
- **No impressions of real broadcasters or players.**
- **No fabricated quotes.** Ever. If a quote is used it is sourced and real.
- **Player criticism stays on performance.** Never character, never appearance, never background.

## 12. Year-1 scope — what is explicitly out

- Sports other than NFL

- Guest-agent tentpole releases

## 13. Kill criteria

Predetermined so sunk cost doesn't make the decision:

| Checkpoint | Continue if | Iterate if | Stop if |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 30** | 1,500+ downloads/ep | 400–1,500 | < 400 |
| **Day 90** | 5,000+ downloads/ep, 30%+ ep-over-ep retention | 1,500–5,000 | < 1,500 |
| **Day 180** | 15,000+ downloads/ep or first paid sponsorship | 5,000–15,000 | < 5,000 |

Production hours are also a kill metric: if an episode still takes more than 5 hours all-in at Month 3, the pipeline has failed regardless of audience.
