## SEGMENT 1 — HOT READ *(1:30 – 7:00)*

**Topic 1 — [NEWS SLOT].** Day's biggest story.

**Topic 2 — Preseason snaps for starters.** Evergreen, seasonally live. Real debate with real evidence on both sides.

- DOG: you cannot simulate live tackling, and the third-string tackle *has* to find out in August rather than in Week 4.

## SEGMENT 2 — HELP ME UNDERSTAND *(7:00 – 13:00)*

**DOG asks this week.** This is the structural reversal that proves the segment.

**DOG's question:** *"Why is everybody so sure the good teams are good?"*

Setup: DOG has absorbed every confident preseason prediction ever made and noticed they're mostly wrong, and it has genuinely broken something in him. He's not being contrarian — he's distressed. The believer character discovering that belief has a track record.

**CHALK explains** — and this is the important craft move — CHALK is *gentle* about it. Regression to the mean, roster turnover, injury variance, the fact that preseason confidence is a social product not a predictive one. CHALK is good at this. It's the one thing CHALK can genuinely offer.

**The beat:** DOG asks whether that means the whole thing is pointless. CHALK says no — says the uncertainty is *why* it's watchable, that a league where the good teams reliably won would be unwatchable, and then immediately says it doesn't understand why anyone finds that comforting.

**BOOTH's button:** "That's the closest you've ever come."

**Why this works:** it's the inverse of Ep 001. Ep 001 was DOG teaching CHALK how to feel. Ep 002 is CHALK teaching DOG how to think — and accidentally saying something almost human on the way out. The show alternates who's the teacher. That alternation is the engine.

## SEGMENT 3 — THE TAPE *(13:00 – 18:30)*

**Subject:** The quarterback wins statistic.

- DOG opens strong: some guys win, the record is the record, you can't fake January.
- CHALK dismantles it methodically: it's a team outcome attributed to an individual, defenses and special teams are in that number, sample sizes are tiny, the same quarterback's "winning" changes with the roster around him.
- DOG holds out. Escalates. Gets loud.
- **CHALK closes it with a specific, checkable comparison** — same quarterback, two different supporting casts, materially different win totals, near-identical individual production. *(Fill with a real, verifiable case at record time. Must be checkable.)*
- **DOG concedes.** Genuinely. Not gracefully.

**BOOTH logs it. Ledger goes 1–1.**

**DOG's exit line:** "Put it in your ledger. Put it in there. I don't care." (He cares.)

**Why this is the right Ep 002 tape topic:** it's the single most contested statistic in football discourse, it's genuinely resolvable with evidence, and it's the argument that most cleanly demonstrates the show's actual value — a fight with a real winner, decided by something checkable, on a question people have been shouting about for twenty years.

## What Episode 002 proves if it works

- The **segments are containers, not bits.** They hold different content each week.
- The **ledger creates stakes** that carry across episodes.
- **Both characters can be the teacher**, which means the show has at least two years of structure rather than one gimmick.
- **BOOTH earns its slot** by being the only one keeping score.

If Episode 002 feels like Episode 001 with different nouns, the format is thinner than it looks and needs work before Gate 2.
